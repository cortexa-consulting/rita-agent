# Client deck

Build a PowerPoint deck a client can read in five minutes, or have summarized
for them by an AI tool, and still get the point.

## Structure

1. **Title:** client, topic, date.
2. **The answer:** three bullets that stand on their own. If the reader only
   sees this slide, or only reads an AI summary of the deck, they have the
   point.
3. **One slide per finding**, biggest first.
4. **What could go wrong / what we are watching.**
5. **The ask:** what you need from the client, by when.

6 to 8 slides unless told otherwise.

## Rules

- **Headlines are full sentences that state the takeaway.** "Back-to-school
  units rose 38% at Best Byte", not "Back-to-school". Put each one in the
  slide's title placeholder, not a free text box, so outline view, screen
  readers and AI summarizers see it.
- **One idea per slide.** Two ideas is two slides.
- **Every number cites its source** in the slide footer: file name and, for a
  spreadsheet, the sheet or rows; for an email, its file name, never just
  its sender.
- **Speaker notes** on every slide: what to say, and the one question the
  client is likely to ask.
- **Follow all of the client's brand guide** if there is one (the account's
  brief says where):
  colors (the ones it names for backgrounds and for the client's own
  brand), type, and its naming and voice rules. Its minimum
  type size covers everything on a slide, footers and chart labels
  included. Otherwise:
  one accent color, plenty of white space, 14pt minimum body text.
- **Charts:** label lines and bars directly; make the client's own brand the
  strongest color and competitors neutral.
- **No hype words.** Numbers over adjectives.
- **Only what the files say.** Cite a fact only to a file that states it,
  and make no promise in the person's name (a new report, a date) that the
  material doesn't show they made.

## Where things go

These lines fit the course workspace (`about-me/`, `projects/<account>/`,
`outputs/`). Skip any part that isn't there. The account is the client the
work is for. `projects/rita-agent/` is the course kit, never an account:
its practice data is for Dalton, its practice client. List `projects/`
first, and never say an account's folder is missing without having listed
it.

- **Read the account first.** When the work is for an account (named, or plain
  from the material, such as Dalton products in an export), list `projects/`
  to find its folder (the practice account is `projects/dalton/`), and read
  its `brief.md` and `memory.md` before you start, and use its `reference/`
  folder (brand guide, glossary, templates). Work for one engagement
  (`projects/<account>/<engagement>/`) reads that folder's `brief.md` and
  `memory.md` too.
- **Write as the person.** Emails and drafts written as them follow
  `about-me/writing-rules.md`; your reply to them doesn't.
- **Save** to `outputs/<account>/` (or `outputs/<account>/<engagement>/`) at
  the top of the workspace, or `outputs/client-deck/` when no account applies,
  unless the person names a place. Without a workspace folder, offer the file
  to download. Say where it is.
- **Offer a memory line.** When you deliver, write out a one- or two-line
  entry for the account's (or engagement's) `memory.md` (what you made, where,
  what's still open) and offer to add it. Write it only on a yes, and keep
  client figures (any number from the data: units, returns, prices, sales),
  contact details and anything confidential out of it.

## Before you deliver

Open the saved .pptx and read every slide's text and footer: check the file,
not your script. Then list the check pass as the last section of your
reply, after the memory line offer: every number traced to its source;
every headline matches its chart; the answer slide agrees with the finding
slides; the brand guide's naming rules are followed on the slides (product
names in full on first use, if it asks); anything estimated is labeled as
an estimate; nothing confidential from another client is in the deck.
