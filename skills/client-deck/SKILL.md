# Client deck

Build a PowerPoint deck a client can read in five minutes, or have summarized
for them by an AI tool, and still get the point.

## Structure

1. **Title:** client, topic, date.
2. **The answer:** three bullets that stand on their own. If the reader only
   sees this slide, or only reads an AI summary of the deck, they have the
   point.
3. **One slide per finding**, biggest first.
4. **What could go wrong / what we are watching.**
5. **The ask:** what you need from the client, by when.
6. **Sources and notes** (appendix, last slide): see Sources below.

6 to 8 slides plus the appendix unless told otherwise. A slide count the
person asks for doesn't include the Sources and notes appendix: add it and
say so.

## The look

A strategy-firm base that the client's brand guide sits on top of. 16:9.

**Font, in this order:** Avenir Next LT Pro (a Microsoft 365 cloud font,
so Office downloads it when the deck opens), then Gotham where the person
says it's installed and licensed, then Avenir, then Segoe UI (on every
Windows PC). **Never Calibri**, even as a fallback; if a brand guide asks
for it, use the first font on this list and say so. A brand guide's own
font, other than Calibri, comes first. Set the font on every text box,
table, chart label and note, so nothing falls back to the theme's. Name
Avenir Next LT Pro in the file (Office downloads it); use Gotham only when
the person says they have it. Set it everywhere a default lives: the theme's
heading and body fonts, the document defaults and the Normal style, not only
the text you write, then check those in the saved file.

- **Title slide:** white, a thin dark band down the left edge, a small
  all-caps label ("DALTON COMPUTERS · Q3 2026 BUSINESS REVIEW") in the
  accent color, the takeaway of the whole deck as one large sentence, a
  short accent rule, then who it's for and the date in gray.
- **Every content slide:** a small all-caps tracker label above the
  headline ("THE ANSWER", "HOW Q3 FINISHED · EXHIBIT 1"), the sentence
  headline (about 26pt, bold, dark navy), and a thin gray rule under it.
  Consistent margins; nothing crowds the edges.
- **The answer slide:** three columns, each a big number (about 40pt), a
  small all-caps label, and one plain line, divided by thin rules.
- **Charts:** state what is measured, and the period, in a gray line above
  the chart; highlight the one bar or line that makes the point, gray the
  rest; label values directly; no gridlines, no value axis, no legend
  when labels will do. Beside each chart, a light gray "So what" panel of
  two or three short lines.
- **Color:** dark navy for headlines, one accent color for labels and
  highlights, grays for everything else. With a brand guide, its colors
  replace the navy and the accent; the layout stays.
- **Type:** the font above throughout; headlines bold; body 14pt or more;
  footers and labels 10pt or more, unless the brand guide sets a larger
  minimum.

## Sources

Three layers, so the slides stay clean and every number can still be
traced.

1. **On the slide:** a small numbered marker (¹ ²) where a sourced number
   appears, and one footer line in gray: "Sources ¹ Dalton sell-through,
   Q3, cleaned (Halcrest) ² Macro Center price correction, Oct 30". Name
   what the source is, in plain words, not its file name. Mark estimates
   "(est.)" where they appear. Slide number at the right.
2. **Sources and notes, the appendix slide:** a table of #, Source, What
   it is and what we did with it, File. The detail lives here: dates and
   ranges, rows removed or corrected, the license on panel or partner data,
   and the method behind every estimate (marked E).
3. **Speaker notes:** the exact trace for the presenter: file, sheet,
   rows or filter.

Link only public sources (a retailer's press release, an earnings report),
in the appendix. Never link files on the person's computer or a shared
drive: the link breaks or exposes internal folders once the deck leaves
the team, and it can hand over data the client may not be allowed to see.

## Rules

- **Headlines are full sentences that state the takeaway.** "Back-to-school
  units rose 38% at Best Byte", not "Back-to-school". Put each one in the
  slide's title placeholder, not a free text box, so outline view, screen
  readers and AI summarizers see it.
- **One idea per slide.** Two ideas is two slides.
- **Write names the brand guide's way from the first draft,** not only at
  the check: product names in full on first use ("Dalton Aero 14", then
  "Aero 14") if it asks, on the title slide too.
- **Every number has a source,** layered as in Sources above. The
  appendix gives the file name and, for a spreadsheet, the sheet or rows;
  for an email, its file name, never just its sender.
- **Speaker notes** on every slide: what to say, and the one question the
  client is likely to ask.
- **Follow all of the client's brand guide** on top of The look, if there
  is one (the account's brief says where):
  colors (the ones it names for backgrounds and for the client's own
  brand), type, and its naming and voice rules. Its minimum
  type size covers everything on a slide, footers and chart labels
  included. Otherwise:
  one accent color, plenty of white space, 14pt minimum body text.
- **Charts:** label lines and bars directly; make the client's own brand the
  strongest color and competitors neutral.
- **No hype words.** Numbers over adjectives.
- **Only what the files say.** Cite a fact only to a file that states it,
  and make no promise in the person's name (a new report, a date) that the
  material doesn't show they made.
- **Make it unmistakable, not just accurate.** A busy reader, or their AI
  summarizer, sees the big numbers and the headlines, so:
  - **Say what the dollars are:** retail sell-through (what shoppers paid),
    net sales, or the client's own revenue. Never a bare "$9.43M".
  - **Every total says its period.** Any slide with a total, a change or a
    share names the weeks or dates it covers, in the headline, the chart
    title or the source line: "13 weeks ending Oct 3", not just "Q3". When
    the data's period differs from the calendar quarter, say so once on the
    answer slide. Slides get forwarded one at a time.
  - **Don't mix kinds of number** in one column, chart or total: an
    estimate, a reported figure and a data correction each get their own
    label, and say when they shouldn't be added.
  - **Caveats that change the meaning go on the slide,** not only in the
    speaker notes: the scope ("at Best Byte and Macro Center"), a
    comparison that flatters, a seasonal dip. Summarizers skip the notes.
  - **Claim no more than the source shows:** data by region can't support
    "the same stores"; two store visits aren't three; and two numbers that
    are about the same size aren't a cause ("about the size of", not
    "matches" or "because of").
- **Licensed and partner data** (retail panels, POS feeds, retailer portal
  exports) comes with terms, marked or not. Before its numbers go on a
  slide, find what its license allows (a note in the file, its folder's
  README, or the account's brief), follow it, and footnote the data as
  licensed. If you can't find the terms, ask; don't assume.

## Where things go

These lines fit the course workspace (`about-me/`, `projects/<account>/`,
`outputs/`). Skip any part that isn't there. The account is the client the
work is for. `projects/rita-agent/` is the course kit, never an account:
its practice data is for Dalton, its practice client. List `projects/`
first, and never say an account's folder is missing without having listed
it.

- **Read the account first.** When the work is for an account (named, or plain
  from the material, such as Dalton products in an export), list `projects/`
  to find its folder (the practice account is `projects/dalton/`), and read
  its `brief.md` and `memory.md` before you start, and use its `reference/`
  folder (brand guide, glossary, templates). Work for one engagement
  (`projects/<account>/<engagement>/`) reads that folder's `brief.md` and
  `memory.md` too.
- **Learn from the sources, not old outputs.** Take the client's style
  and rules from its `brief.md` and `reference/`, not by copying an earlier
  file in `outputs/`. If an old deliverable is the best example, suggest
  putting a copy in `reference/`.
- **Write as the person.** Emails and drafts written as them follow
  `about-me/writing-rules.md`; your reply to them doesn't.
- **Save** to `outputs/<account>/` (or `outputs/<account>/<engagement>/`) at
  the top of the workspace, or `outputs/client-deck/` when no account applies,
  unless the person names a place. Without a workspace folder, offer the file
  to download. Say where it is.
- **Working files stay out of the workspace.** Build scripts, test renders
  and scratch copies go in a temporary folder outside it, or are deleted when
  you finish; only what you deliver lands in `outputs/`.
- **Offer a memory line.** When you deliver, write out a one- or two-line
  entry for the account's (or engagement's) `memory.md` (what you made, where,
  what's still open) and offer to add it. Write it only on a yes, and keep
  client figures (any number from the data: units, returns, prices, sales),
  contact details and anything confidential out of it.

## Before you deliver

Open the saved .pptx and read every slide's text, footer and the appendix:
check the file, not your script. Render the slides and look at them too:
nothing overlaps, crowds an edge or falls below the brand guide's type
minimum (footers and labels included), and no text uses Calibri. Fix what
the check finds before you deliver, and report only what the saved file
shows. A number on the title slide carries its source marker too. Then list
the check pass as the last section of your reply, after the memory line
offer: every number traced to its source; every headline matches its chart;
the answer slide agrees with the finding slides; the brand guide's naming
rules are followed on the slides (product names in full on first use, if it
asks); anything estimated is labeled as an estimate; dollars say what they
are; every total names its period; no column or total mixes estimates,
reported figures and corrections; caveats that change the meaning are on the
slides; no claim goes beyond its source; nothing confidential from another
client is in the deck.
